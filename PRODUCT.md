# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Intermediate Khmer learners reading real Khmer websites (news, Wikipedia,
Facebook). They reach the options page occasionally, to set the popup to
their taste (theme, size, whether to show romanization). Their real work
happens on the web page, not here — this surface should be quick to
understand and quick to leave.

## Product Purpose

KhmerLens is a hover dictionary. The options page configures how the
lookup popup looks and behaves. Success is a settings screen a learner
can scan in seconds, change one thing with confidence, and see the effect
without guessing — the popup they're tuning is the thing they should see.

## Brand Personality

Calm, literate, precise. A study tool, not a toy. The Khmer script is the
hero; chrome stays quiet around it. Three words: scholarly, warm, exact.

## Anti-references

Generic Chrome-extension options pages (a stack of unstyled native
checkboxes on white). Gamified language-app dashboards with mascots,
streaks, and confetti. Heavy SaaS settings shells with sidebars the
content doesn't need.

## Design Principles

- **Show the thing being configured.** A live popup preview beats prose
  descriptions of what each toggle does.
- **Earned familiarity.** Standard settings affordances (toggles,
  segmented controls) tuned well, not reinvented.
- **The script leads.** Khmer type is the one place to spend visual
  weight; everything else recedes.
- **Quick in, quick out.** No scroll-hunting; the controls sit above the
  fold, reference material below.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA: body text ≥4.5:1 in both themes, visible keyboard focus on every
control, toggles operable by keyboard with correct ARIA state, respects
`prefers-reduced-motion` and `prefers-color-scheme`.
